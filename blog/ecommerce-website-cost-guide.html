How Much Does an Ecommerce Website Cost in 2026?

By the DigitalDot Web Development Team • Published November 13, 2026 • 11 min read

Every week, a small business owner asks us some version of the same question: "how much does an ecommerce website cost?" It's a fair question with a frustrating answer, because the honest range runs from under $50 a month to well over $20,000 upfront — and the quotes you'll get from different vendors often look like they're pricing completely different products. That's because they usually are.

At DigitalDot, we scope and build ecommerce stores for US small businesses regularly, and this guide breaks down ecommerce website cost the way we actually price projects — by platform, by scope, and by the recurring costs that show up after launch and quietly change the real total. No inflated "starting at" numbers, no padding.

What actually drives ecommerce website cost

Before comparing numbers, it helps to understand why ecommerce website cost varies so much more than a brochure site. A basic business website is mostly pages and content. An ecommerce store is a transactional application — it has to handle inventory, tax calculation, shipping rates, payment processing, and customer accounts, and each of those adds real engineering and configuration time. The five biggest cost drivers we see when scoping a project are:

  • Product catalog size and complexity. 20 simple products with one variant each is a very different build than 500 products with size, color, and custom-option variants.
  • Payment and tax setup. Connecting a payment processor is quick; configuring multi-state US sales tax correctly is not, and getting it wrong creates real liability.
  • Custom design vs. a theme. A customized storefront that matches your brand costs more than a lightly modified off-the-shelf theme, but it also converts better and is harder for competitors to copy.
  • Integrations. Connecting inventory to a POS system, syncing to accounting software, or adding a subscription model all add scope.
  • Content and photography. Product photography, copywriting, and category structure are frequently underestimated line items that affect launch timelines as much as price.

The hidden costs most quotes leave out

The upfront build price is only part of the real ecommerce website cost. Here's what regularly gets left out of a first quote and shows up later:

  1. Payment processing fees. Typically 2.6%–3.5% per transaction — a real, ongoing cost that scales with your revenue, not a flat fee.
  2. Platform and app fees. Shopify-style platforms charge a monthly subscription, and most stores add 3–6 paid apps for reviews, email, or upsells that each carry their own monthly cost.
  3. PCI compliance. Any site accepting card payments has to meet payment security standards; the PCI Security Standards Council publishes the requirements, and most hosted platforms handle the bulk of this for you, but self-hosted setups need it budgeted explicitly.
  4. Ongoing maintenance. Security patches, plugin updates, and performance monitoring don't stop after launch — see our website maintenance breakdown for what that should realistically cost.
  5. SEO and content. A store with zero organic visibility needs ongoing category and product-page SEO work; Google's own SEO Starter Guide is a solid baseline for what that work should cover technically.

A small business ecommerce website that looks like a $5,000 one-time cost on paper often runs $150–$400 a month in fees, apps, and processing once it's actually live and selling.

DIY platforms vs. a custom-built store

Shopify-style DIY platforms are a genuinely good option for a straightforward catalog with standard checkout needs — a shopify ecommerce website cost of $29–$79/month plus a templated theme can get a real store live in days, not weeks. Where DIY platforms hit a ceiling is custom checkout logic, complex product configurators, non-standard shipping rules, or a design that needs to stand out rather than look like every other store on the same theme.

A custom ecommerce website costs more upfront because you're paying for development time instead of a monthly license, but you own the design outright, aren't boxed in by a theme's limitations, and typically see better page speed — which matters directly at checkout. Google's Core Web Vitals research consistently links slower load times to higher abandonment, and that effect compounds on a multi-step checkout flow where every extra second of friction has a direct, measurable revenue cost.

The real cost of an ecommerce website isn't the invoice you pay at launch — it's the conversion rate you're stuck with for the next three years.

How to get a quote you can actually trust

Because ecommerce website cost varies so much by scope, the quote itself matters less than what's behind it. Before accepting any proposal, ask for:

  • An exact product and variant count the quote assumes — catalog size is the single biggest hidden scope-creep source.
  • A clear line between one-time build cost and recurring monthly cost (hosting, apps, payment fees).
  • Who owns the theme, code, and content after the project ends.
  • Whether product photography, copywriting, and category structure are included or billed separately.
  • What happens after launch — see our small business website design guide for the same due-diligence questions applied to non-ecommerce sites, most of which transfer directly.

Mistakes that quietly inflate the price

1. Scoping the catalog late

Starting a build before finalizing product count and variants almost always causes expensive rework once real data gets loaded in.

2. Choosing a platform based on price alone

The cheapest monthly plan often caps out on products, storage, or staff accounts well before a growing store needs it — forcing a costly mid-year migration.

3. Treating photography as an afterthought

Stock or low-quality product images undercut a site's design no matter how much was spent building it. Budget for real photography from day one.

4. Skipping tax and shipping configuration review

Misconfigured multi-state tax rules create compliance risk that costs far more to fix later than to set up correctly at launch.

5. No plan for ongoing SEO

A custom ecommerce website cost that excludes any SEO plan is a store that's invisible on launch day and stays that way without continued work.

Frequently asked questions

What is a realistic small business ecommerce website cost?

For a catalog of 20–200 products with a custom-styled storefront on a platform like Shopify, expect $4,000–$8,000 upfront plus $100–$250/month in platform, app, and processing fees.

Is a custom ecommerce website worth the extra cost over Shopify?

It's worth it when you have checkout logic, product configuration, or integrations a theme can't handle, or when design differentiation is a real competitive factor. For a standard catalog with standard checkout, a well-built Shopify store is usually the more cost-effective choice.

Does ecommerce website cost include ongoing maintenance?

Rarely, unless explicitly stated. Always ask whether a quote is one-time-only or includes a maintenance period, and budget separately for the recurring platform, app, and processing costs covered above.
